# Phase 1A smoke result — 2026-08-01

Verdict: **GO.** Tenant signal is present in trace structure alone, the leakage canary is
clean across five seeds, and Phase 1B is worth building.

> This document was rewritten after the timing ablation below. The first version headlined
> 0.266 accuracy (5.9x chance). That figure included three session-timing columns which
> carry ~40% of the excess accuracy and reflect serving-endpoint latency rather than
> contributor behaviour. The headline below excludes them. The earlier framing is
> preserved as the leaky-metadata variant, because the comparison is itself the finding.

Baselines are **measured**, not nominal. At 0.1% FPR the empirical null is 0.002 rather
than the nominal 0.001, so the honest lift there is 14.5x and not the 29x a nominal
baseline would have implied.

## The timing ablation, which is a finding in its own right

`duration_s`, `steps_per_minute` and `has_duration` are 3 columns of 2663. Removing them:

| Regime | Accuracy | TPR @ 1% FPR | TPR @ 0.1% FPR |
|---|---|---|---|
| With timing | 0.284 [0.255-0.301] | 0.147 [0.126-0.165] | 0.044 [0.032-0.055] |
| Structure only | 0.187 [0.171-0.208] | 0.087 [0.073-0.098] | 0.029 [0.022-0.039] |

Both regimes, five seeds each. Excess accuracy over the majority baseline is 0.237 with
timing and 0.140 without, so **41% of the signal was wall-clock latency** — and a larger
share still of the confident identifications at low FPR (0.147 to 0.087 at 1% FPR).

Session duration is dominated by the serving endpoint's speed and load at collection time — the same defect
for which absolute timestamps were already excluded, at finer grain. For the real threat
model (a company pooling its traces) duration is both the least organization-specific
signal available and the most trivially strippable.

The per-tenant ranking reorders once it is removed, which is the clearest evidence it was
an artifact. `grok-4-fast` sits 4th with timing (0.419) and 16th without (0.136): it was
being recognised for a 70-second median session, not for how it works.
`claude-4.5-haiku-1001` falls from 11th (0.258) to last (0.072) the same way. The tenants
that hold their position — `gemini-2.5-flash` 1st in both, `o4-mini` and
`deepseek-3.2-thinking` in the top three of both — are the ones identified by behaviour.

## Per-tenant recall — the distribution is the result

| Band | Tenants | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| Strongly identified | gemini-2.5-flash 0.479, o4-mini 0.356, deepseek-3.2-thinking 0.323 | 0.32-0.48 |
| Moderately | qwen-3-coder 0.269, gemini-2.5-pro 0.266, o3 0.236, gpt-5.1 0.223 | 0.22-0.27 |
| Weakly | grok-code-fast-1, minimax-m2, gpt-5-high, grok-4, gemini-3-pro-preview, gpt-5-mini, claude-4.5-opus, gpt-5, grok-4-fast, claude-4.5-sonnet-0929, deepseek-v3.2-exp | 0.10-0.18 |
| Near-invisible | claude-4-sonnet-0514 0.098, kimi-k2-0905 0.093, glm-4.6 0.079, claude-4.5-haiku-1001 0.072 | 0.07-0.10 |

Following Carlini et al. (arXiv:2112.03570), the mean is the wrong summary: an attack can
look mild on average while identifying some subjects reliably. Here it identifies roughly a
third of tenants well and barely touches the rest.

For a marketplace this is the sharper warning than the average would be. Pooling protects
most suppliers and singles out a few, and a supplier cannot be told in advance which group
they are in.

## Coverage, and how attrition is distributed

307 of 7185 records skipped (4.3%), every one counted, none silently dropped: 113 carry
`"messages": null` (runs that never completed), ~100 are unparseable JSON, and the
remainder contain no tool calls.

Attrition is **not uniform across tenants** and this was undisclosed in the first version:

| Highest | Rate | Lowest | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| o3 | 12.9% | grok-4-fast | 0.0% |
| kimi-k2-0905 | 9.8% | deepseek-3.2-thinking | 0.3% |
| o4-mini | 9.5% | glm-4.6 | 0.3% |
| gpt-5-mini | 8.5% | gemini-3-pro-preview | 0.6% |
| gpt-5-high, gemini-2.5-flash | 8.3% | claude-4.5-haiku-1001, claude-4.5-opus | 1.5% |

A 40x spread, skewed toward reasoning models. Every category is behaviour-driven, and
sessions with zero tool calls — the most distinctive sessions a tenant can produce — are
removed. The bias therefore runs **conservative**: it deletes distinctive behaviour and
deflates the measured effect. Two of the three best-identified tenants
(gemini-2.5-flash 8.3%, o4-mini 9.5%) are also high-attrition, so the effect is probably
understated for exactly the tenants that matter most.

## Precision limits

- The 2108 test sessions come from 33 held-out tasks x 22 tenants x ~3 runs. The three runs
  of one (tenant, task) pair are near-replicates, so the independent unit is the task and
  the naive binomial standard error understates the true spread. The five-seed range is the
  honest interval, not the point estimate.
- TPR @ 0.1% FPR sits near its resolution floor: with ~94 positives per class the smallest
  non-zero per-class value is 1/94 = 0.0106. The reported 0.029 is roughly three traces per
  tenant. It should not be read to three significant figures.

## Corrections to claims made in the first version

- **"No text crosses the `features/` boundary."** Text is stripped in the *adapter*; one
  derived bit per span (`ok`/`error`) crosses, produced by a keyword scan over tool-output
  text. `features/` is where text is absent, not where it is removed. Separately, a
  malformed hyphen-less tool id previously reached `feature_names` carrying ~1.4 KB of raw
  model chain-of-thought. Feature-name tokens are now constrained and violators collapse to
  `malformed`; the bound on how much this could have inflated the result was ≤1.4 accuracy
  points.
- **"Identical tool registry across tenants."** At depth 1, 63 of 98 tokens are
  tenant-exclusive, almost all model-emitted malformations (`canvas_list_courses` for
  `canvas-list_courses`, truncations like `rail_1`, the typo `howtocoook`). The taxonomy
  normalizes 99.85% of spans; the rest are raw identifiers.
- **"The shuffled control shows there is no pipeline leakage."** The permutation happens
  after `extract()`, so it validates the classifier and the split and cannot detect anything
  during feature construction. The n-gram vocabulary is fit transductively over train and
  test together; this was separately verified numerically inert (557 of 2643 n-gram columns
  have zero train support, so no tree can split on them), but the control did not establish
  that.
- **`error_rate` is a keyword scan**, not a structural field. It matches
  `error|traceback|failed|fail:|exception|denied` in the first 200 characters of tool output
  and has demonstrable false positives, including JSON envelopes containing `"error": null`
  and a file named for passed/failed test counts. It carries η²=0.067 and is not driving the
  result, but it is a text-derived signal.

## Excluded by construction, before any run

Token counts and cost (`agent_cost`, `key_stats`) encode per-model pricing and tokenizer
identity. They would separate tenants near-perfectly while corresponding to nothing that
identifies a company. Absolute timestamps are excluded because collection era correlates
with tenant. Session duration is now excluded from the headline for the same reason.

More tenants lowers chance from 1/17 to 1/22 and makes the closed-set problem harder.

## Effect size is a lower bound on field conditions

Toolathlon's tenant is the model, and every tenant runs the same tasks against the same
tools. Real companies differ additionally in tool stack, prompt style, task mix and model.
Separating models under held-constant conditions is the hardest version of the problem, so
4.0x here should be read as a floor, not a ceiling.

Prior work reports 90-98% F1 for agent fingerprinting, but consumes far richer signal: UI
event timings (arXiv:2605.14786), encrypted packet traces (arXiv:2510.07176), raw shell
command text (arXiv:2605.01186), commit and PR structure (arXiv:2601.17406). This run uses
namespace-level sequence structure with no timing and no text.

## What this does not show

- Nothing about whether value and privacy risk correlate at tenant level. That is Phase 1B
  and the open question the project exists to answer.
- Nothing about a real company boundary. The tenant here is a model, a conservative proxy.
- Nothing about open-set or unsupervised partition, which is the commercially harmful
  attack. This run is closed-set only.
- The distillation-convergence risk (arXiv:2604.21255) is untested.
- Depth 0 has not been run: the taxonomy maps 23 of 98 observed namespaces, so 6.6% of spans
  would collapse to `other`. The table needs extending before depth 0 means anything.
- Depth 2 was attempted and abandoned after two hours on a single split; full tool ids
  explode the n-gram vocabulary far beyond the 2660 columns at depth 1.
